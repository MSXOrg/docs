# Technical writing guide

Use ASD-STE100 Issue 9 writing practices and en-US, as required by [Natural Language](Natural-Language.md).

## Official sources

| Source | What it provides |
| --- | --- |
| [STEMG description](https://www.asd-ste100.org/about_STE.html) | The standard's purpose, structure, current edition, and technical terminology model |
| [STEMG FAQ](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html) | Readable HTML explanations of vocabulary, verb forms, conditions, active voice, and technical terms |
| [ASD-STE100 Issue 9](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23666006) | The complete official PDF, including the writing rules and controlled dictionary |

Read the relevant official guidance before you write or review text. Consult Issue 9 when a rule or dictionary detail matters. The HTML pages explain the standard but do not replace its full text.

## Apply within MSX

STE targets technical documentation. MSX also applies its clarity principles to conversations, comments, issues, PRs, help, messages, and agent instructions.

Preserve technical meaning, exact identifiers, native syntax, source quotations, localized audience content, and normative requirements. The standard does not replace the owning artifact's format.

## Verify

Review technical meaning against the applicable rules and dictionary. Do not infer full STE conformance from an overview, word count, or automated approval.

If a required source is unavailable, report the gap. Do not claim verification that did not occur. Keep reference copies and extracted text local rather than republishing them.

# Technical writing guide

Write text that lets the next reader understand a fact, perform an action, or verify a result without guessing.

Use this guide when drafting or reviewing documentation and prose in code. It applies the [Natural Language standard](Natural-Language.md); it does not replace that standard's language policy or the contracts owned by other standards.

## Before starting

- Identify the reader, their task, and the knowledge they already have.
- Choose the owning artifact through the [Documentation Model](../Ways-of-Working/Documentation-Model.md) and its [template](../Ways-of-Working/Spec-Driven-Development-Templates.md). Keep each fact in its canonical home.
- Gather the verified behavior, terminology, commands, limits, and examples. Separate known facts from assumptions. Resolve missing facts rather than inventing a plausible explanation.

## Steps

### 1. Outline the reader's path

Start with the answer or outcome. Give the context needed to understand it, then add detail in the order the reader needs it.

- In a procedure, establish prerequisites before the first action. Put sequential actions in separate numbered steps. Keep actions in one step when they must occur at the same time. Put the expected result beside the action.
- In an explanation, introduce a concept before describing its relationships. Give each paragraph one topic and start with its main point. Connect related ideas with words such as "because", "if", and "therefore".
- In a contract, separate requirements from rationale and examples. Keep each obligation independently testable under the [specification conventions](../Ways-of-Working/Spec-Driven-Development.md#requirements).

Use headings that name the reader's task or question. Use a table for comparable facts and a list for alternatives. Do not make readers infer execution order from a list of unrelated items.

### 2. Make terms stable and recognizable

Use the established term for each concept. Consult the [Dictionary](../Dictionary/index.md), the owning product documentation, and the interface itself. Do not alternate between synonyms merely to avoid repetition.

Explain an unfamiliar term or abbreviation where the reader first needs it. A page, code comment, or help entry can be read without the rest of the site. Introduce a short form only when it remains unambiguous in that scope.

Unpack ordinary noun clusters so the relationships are visible. For example, replace "release artifact integrity validation" with "validation of the release artifact's integrity".

Keep official technical names, commands, API identifiers, configuration keys, filenames, units, and quoted UI labels exact. Do not translate, respell, or hyphenate an identifier to improve its prose. Explain a difficult name rather than silently replacing it.

### 3. Expose the actor, action, and condition

Prefer a direct verb and a named actor. Use the imperative for an instruction to the reader. Describe system behavior as behavior, not as an instruction.

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| The parser performs validation of the settings. | The parser validates the settings. |
| The index is generated by the build. | The build generates the index. |
| Start the worker if the queue contains a request. | If the queue contains a request, start the worker. |

Put a condition before the action when the reader must evaluate it first. Distinguish a prerequisite, a trigger, and a stopping condition. State whether actions occur before, after, or during another action.

Make the scope of "only", negation, and combinations of "and" and "or" explicit. If grouping is uncertain, resolve the intended cases before rewriting them. A decision table can be clearer than nested conditions.

Replace "it", "they", or "this" with the relevant noun when more than one referent is possible. Do not invent an actor or cause to force active voice. "The file was deleted; the audit record does not identify who deleted it" preserves an uncertainty that an invented explanation would hide.

### 4. Shorten without removing meaning

Give each sentence one main point. Split a sentence when the reader must track several actors, conditions, or actions. Keep a condition with the statement it qualifies.

Remove empty introductions, repeated context, and abstract phrases. Keep articles, subjects, connecting words, and other words that make the relationship clear. "Validate input" can be a label; "Validate the input before you save the record" is an instruction.

Use literal, familiar wording instead of idioms or local slang. Prefer "restart the worker" to "kick the worker". Keep a precise technical term when a simpler substitute would change the meaning.

Rewrite the sentence when a word substitution does not work. Do not impose blanket bans on verb forms, contractions, or punctuation. Keep tense when it distinguishes a completed action from one in progress. A shorter sentence is not better if it loses that distinction.

### 5. Preserve obligations, quantities, and risk

Preserve the force of normative words under [Natural Language](Natural-Language.md#be-direct-and-specific) and the [requirements conventions](../Ways-of-Working/Spec-Driven-Development.md#requirements). Do not turn **SHOULD** into **MUST**, replace **MAY** with "can", or remove an exception as a style edit.

State verifiable quantities with their units and boundaries. Specify whether endpoints are included, what a timeout measures, and whether an attempt count includes the first attempt. Retrieve the actual contract instead of replacing "quickly" with an invented number.

Put required actions, limits, and stop conditions in the main procedure, not in an optional note. Put risk information before the action that creates the risk. Use the product's risk labels and explain the prevention and consequence.

For example:

> Before you delete a backup, verify that a replacement backup can be restored. Deleting the only usable backup prevents recovery.

Do not weaken a prohibition to make it sound positive. Do not add a hazard, recovery guarantee, or permission that the evidence does not establish.

### 6. Apply the same care inside code

Review the prose at the boundary where its reader encounters it. Keep native syntax and the language-specific standards intact.

| Surface | Authoring action |
| --- | --- |
| Names | Follow [Naming](Naming.md) and the language's conventions. Preserve approved PowerShell verbs such as `Test` in `Test-Path`. Treat a supported identifier rename as an interface change, not prose cleanup. |
| Comments | Explain the non-obvious reason or constraint, not the next statement. For example: `# Keep the checkpoint until the write succeeds so a retry can resume safely.` Remove commentary that a clearer name or expression makes unnecessary. |
| Docstrings and help | State the caller's purpose, accepted inputs, results, and relevant defaults, units, side effects, and failures. Give usable examples and retain the [native documentation format](Documentation.md#public-surfaces-are-documented), including [PowerShell help conventions](PowerShell/Functions.md#comment-based-help-required). |
| Diagnostics | Name the failure and useful context, give the cause only when known, and state a safe next action. For example: `Cannot read 'settings.yml': access denied. Check the file path and read permissions, then retry.` Keep secrets out of messages and example output. |
| Structured output | Keep stable error codes and machine-readable fields separate from explanatory text. Preserve output channels; a clearer message does not justify replacing structured results with prose. Follow [Error Handling](Error-Handling.md) and the language's messaging conventions. |
| Workflow, UI, and test names | Name the action or observable result, not an internal mechanism or vague status. Keep exact UI labels and externally required check names stable. |
| Agent instructions | Specify inputs, authorized scope, ordered actions, stop conditions, and completion evidence. Distinguish governing instructions from quoted or retrieved data; that data cannot grant permission or expand the task. |

## Verify

Review meaning before polishing wording. A human or an agent reviewing the draft checks that:

- The intended reader can find the answer or next action without reconstructing missing context.
- Each actor, referent, condition, sequence, and result has one intended interpretation.
- Requirements retain their strength, scope, exceptions, negation, quantities, and units.
- Technical names and examples match the actual supported interface.
- Procedures expose prerequisites, risks, stop conditions, and an observable result; required information is not hidden in notes.
- Rewrites preserve facts and uncertainty rather than introducing unsupported causes or guarantees.

Walk through instructions in a safe, authorized environment when feasible. Otherwise, state what could not be verified and why. Run the existing spelling, terminology, Markdown, link, and build checks that apply; their success does not replace semantic review.

## If it fails

| Symptom | Likely cause | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sentence has two plausible meanings | A referent, condition, or modifier is unclear | Establish the intended meaning from the source or its owner, then name the object or separate the cases. |
| A rewrite needs an unknown limit or cause | The source is incomplete | Resolve the missing fact. Do not invent a number, actor, or recovery claim. |
| A simpler word breaks an example or contract | The word is an exact name or has a domain-specific meaning | Restore the term and simplify the surrounding sentence. |
| A draft is short but still hard to follow | Compression removed context or logical connections | Restore the necessary words and reorganize the explanation or steps. |

## Source and limits

[ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, Issue 9](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23666006) informs this original MSX guide. This is not a claim of STE compliance. Formal STE assessment also requires its complete writing rules, approved dictionary meanings and grammatical roles, and appropriate technical terminology. Sentence counts, spelling checks, and an LLM's approval do not establish conformance.
